# Writing the launch first

*Amazon's way to test an idea before building it: write the press release and the questions first. Written for this
app in our own words from Colin Bryar and Bill Carr's PR/FAQ guide
(https://www.workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq) and Werner Vogels's 2006 post "Working Backwards"
(https://www.allthingsdistributed.com/2006/11/working_backwards.html).*

## Why write it first

If you cannot describe the product simply to a customer, you do not yet know what you are building. Writing the launch
announcement forces the customer, the problem and the benefit into plain words before any work is spent. Vogels's
order: press release, then the FAQ, then the customer experience, then the user manual.

## The press release, part by part

- Heading: the product's name, in words a reader understands.
- Subheading: who the customer is and what they get, in one sentence.
- Summary: city, date, what launched and why it matters.
- Problem: the customer's pain, from their side.
- Solution: how it solves the pain, and why someone would switch from what they use today.
- Two quotes: one from the company, one from a customer.
- How to get started.

## Plain words

Short, clear, customer-facing. Nothing the product will not do at launch. No adjectives a number could replace.

## External questions

What a customer or reporter would ask: the price, how it works, how to get help, where to buy.

## Internal questions

What your own team and investors would ask: the alternatives and why they lose; the size of the market; what new
capabilities you need; what you depend on others for; legal and regulatory issues; the unit economics; and the top
reasons it will not succeed. The guide lists twenty or more such topics.

## Reading it together

Amazon teams read the document in silence first, 15 to 20 minutes with notes in the margin, then discuss for about
40. The goal is to find the truth and improve the idea, not to sell it or to win approval. A first draft takes hours;
a good one can take many rounds.

## After the PR/FAQ

Vogels lists two more documents once the press release and FAQ hold up: the customer experience, told as stories or
mock-ups of how someone uses the product to solve the problem, in precise detail; and the user manual, with the
concepts, how-to steps and reference a customer will need. If either is hard to write, the product is not yet clear.

## Signs the document is not ready

- The problem paragraph describes the founder's frustration, not the customer's.
- The solution lists features without saying why anyone would switch.
- The internal FAQ has no hard questions, or "the top reasons it will fail" are soft.
- Numbers appear without working or links.

(These signs are this app's reading of the guide, not a list from it.)
