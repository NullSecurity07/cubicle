# Documents that moved capital

*A reading list for a founder about to pitch. Inspired by the Billion Dollar PDFs directory,
https://billiondollarpdf.com/ (after Jeremy Giffon's term "billion-dollar PDF": a memo, paper, essay or deck that
gave people a new way to see something and changed where money went). The selection and every summary below are our
own words. Each link goes to the original publisher's page; nothing is copied or re-hosted here.*

---

## VC doctrine

- **How to Start a Startup** — Paul Graham, 2005, VC doctrine — a startup comes down to good people, a thing
  customers want, and spending as little as possible until it works. https://paulgraham.com/start.html
- **RIP Good Times** — Sequoia Capital, 2008, VC doctrine — in a downturn, cut costs early, stretch the runway and get
  to cash-flow positive; do not wait for the market to come back. https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/rip-good-times/
- **Why Software Is Eating the World** — Marc Andreessen, 2011, VC doctrine — every industry is turning into a
  software business, and software companies will take the incumbents' profits.
  https://a16z.com/why-software-is-eating-the-world/
- **All Markets Are Not Created Equal** — Bill Gurley, 2012, VC doctrine — ten traits that make a market worth
  backing, from how often customers buy to how locked in they get.
  https://abovethecrowd.com/2012/11/13/all-markets-are-not-created-equal-10-factors-to-consider-when-evaluating-digital-marketplaces/
- **How to Get Startup Ideas** — Paul Graham, 2012, VC doctrine — the best ideas come from noticing a problem you
  have, not from sitting down to think of one; a few people wanting it badly beats many wanting it a little.
  https://paulgraham.com/startupideas.html
- **Do Things That Don't Scale** — Paul Graham, 2013, VC doctrine — recruit the first users by hand and serve them
  absurdly well; the growth engine comes later. https://paulgraham.com/ds.html
- **Competition Is for Losers** — Peter Thiel, 2014, VC doctrine — lasting profit comes from a monopoly on something
  new, not from fighting in a crowded market. https://www.wsj.com/articles/peter-thiel-competition-is-for-losers-1410535536
- **Aggregation Theory** — Ben Thompson, 2015, VC doctrine — on the internet, whoever owns the customer relationship
  and aggregates demand wins over whoever owns supply. https://stratechery.com/2015/aggregation-theory/
- **The New Moats** — Jerry Chen, 2017, VC doctrine — software alone is easy to copy; defensibility now comes from
  data, workflow and being the system of record. https://greylock.com/greymatter/the-new-moats/
- **Invisible Asymptotes** — Eugene Wei, 2018, VC doctrine — every product has a ceiling on its growth that nobody
  sees yet; find it early and work to lift it. https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2018/5/21/invisible-asymptotes
- **1,000 True Fans** — Kevin Kelly, 2008, VC doctrine — a creator can make a living from a thousand people who buy
  everything they make. https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/

## Technical papers

- **Information Management: A Proposal** — Tim Berners-Lee, 1989, technical paper — linked documents on a network
  would let a whole organisation share what it knows; it became the web.
  https://www.w3.org/History/1989/proposal.html
- **The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine** — Sergey Brin and Lawrence Page, 1998, technical
  paper — rank pages by who links to them and search gets much better. http://infolab.stanford.edu/~backrub/google.html
- **Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System** — Satoshi Nakamoto, 2008, technical paper — money that moves
  between two people with no bank in the middle, kept honest by proof-of-work. https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf
- **Attention Is All You Need** — Vaswani et al., 2017, technical paper — a model built only on attention, the
  Transformer, beats older designs on language tasks and trains faster. https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762
- **Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models** — Kaplan et al., 2020, technical paper — language models improve
  predictably as data, size and compute grow, which made huge training budgets a bet worth placing.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361

## AI essays

- **Software 2.0** — Andrej Karpathy, 2017, AI essay — more and more software will be learned from data rather than
  written by hand. https://karpathy.medium.com/software-2-0-a64152b37c35
- **The Bitter Lesson** — Rich Sutton, 2019, AI essay — general methods that use more computation keep beating
  clever hand-built knowledge. http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html

## Worldviews

- **Increasing Returns and the New World of Business** — W. Brian Arthur, 1996, worldview — in knowledge markets
  the leader tends to pull further ahead, so early lock-in matters more than efficiency.
  https://hbr.org/1996/07/increasing-returns-and-the-new-world-of-business
- **The Secret Tesla Motors Master Plan (just between you and me)** — Elon Musk, 2006, worldview — start with an
  expensive car for few buyers and use the money to make cheaper cars for many.
  https://www.tesla.com/blog/secret-tesla-motors-master-plan-just-between-you-and-me

## Macro memos

- **Buy American. I Am.** — Warren Buffett, 2008, macro memo — in the middle of the crash, fear makes good companies
  cheap, and that is when to buy. https://www.nytimes.com/2008/10/17/opinion/17buffett.html
- **Sea Change** — Howard Marks, 2022, macro memo — the long era of near-zero interest rates is over, and what worked
  in it may not work next. https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/sea-change

## Pitch decks

- **LinkedIn Series B pitch to Greylock** — Reid Hoffman, 2004, pitch deck — a network of professionals is worth
  more as it grows; the deck was later published with Hoffman's notes on why each slide was there.
  https://www.reidhoffman.org/linkedin-pitch-to-greylock/
- **A Proposal to American Research and Development** — Ken Olsen and Harlan Anderson, 1957, pitch deck — the plan
  that founded Digital Equipment Corporation: a small, cheaper computer for more users; kept by the Computer History
  Museum. https://www.computerhistory.org/pdp-1/_media/pdf/DEC.pdp_1.1957.102664472.pdf

## Sector reports

- **Internet Trends 2019** — Mary Meeker, 2019, sector report — the yearly numbers on internet users, usage and
  business models that investors used to size markets. https://www.bondcap.com/report/pdf/Internet_Trends_2019.pdf

## What investors listen for

1. **Why you.** What you have lived, built or seen that others in the room have not.
2. **Why now.** What changed in the last year or two (a cost, a law, a technology, a habit) that makes this possible
   today and not five years ago.
3. **Who exactly.** A customer you can name and find, not "SMBs" or "Gen Z".
4. **Evidence over adjectives.** Users, revenue, retention, a waiting list, a pilot: one real number beats a page of
   promise.
5. **What you learned from users.** Something that surprised you and changed the product.
6. **Market size, bottom up.** Number of customers you can reach × what each pays, not "1% of a $50B market".
7. **How you get customers.** The channel that works today, what a customer costs you, and why it keeps working.
8. **What stops a copy.** Data, workflow, network, brand or speed: the reason a bigger company cannot simply ship it.
9. **The unit economics.** What a customer pays over their life against what it costs to win and serve them.
10. **The ask.** How much, for how long, to reach which milestone that makes the next round easy.
