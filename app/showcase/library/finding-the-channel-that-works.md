# Finding the channel that works

*How to choose where customers will come from. Written for this app in our own words from the Bullseye framework in
Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares's book Traction, as summarised by Nat Eliason
(https://www.nateliason.com/notes/traction-gabriel-weinberg-justin-mares).*

## Half and half

Weinberg and Mares's rule: spend half your time on the product and half on getting traction. Founders who build first
and look for customers later lose months.

## The nineteen channels

Viral marketing; public relations; unconventional PR (stunts, customer appreciation); search engine marketing;
social and display ads; offline ads; search engine optimisation; content marketing; email marketing; engineering as
marketing (free tools that bring people in); targeting blogs; business development (partnerships); sales; affiliate
programs; existing platforms (app stores, marketplaces, big sites' ecosystems); trade shows; offline events; speaking
engagements; community building.

## One idea per channel

Most founders only consider the channels they already know. Write one concrete idea for every channel, including the
ones that feel wrong. The best channel is often one your competitors are ignoring.

## Three rings

Draw three rings. The outer ring is long shots, the middle is what might work, the inner is the three most promising.
Move channels between rings as you learn.

## Cut to three

Never test more than three at once. More than three and none gets a fair test.

## Reading a test

Tests are cheap and short. Their job is not to grow the company yet, only to show whether there is potential. Every
test must answer three things: how much it costs to win a customer this way, how many such customers there are, and
whether they are the customers you want. Write the pass line before the test.

## Focus

Pick the one channel that clearly won and put your traction time into it until it stops improving. The right channel
changes as the company grows: what works for the first hundred customers is rarely what works for the next ten
thousand. When it plateaus, run the Bullseye again.

## Designing a cheap test

Keep the budget small and the time short: a few hundred dollars of ads, one guest post, a handful of sales emails, one
free tool page. Pick the smallest version that can still produce the three numbers. (The dollar sizes here are this
app's suggestion; the book's rule is only that tests be cheap and fast.)

## Traps

- Testing one channel at a time, in a row, so a year passes before you find the one that works.
- Judging a test on clicks or sign-ups instead of cost per real customer.
- Keeping a favourite channel in the inner ring after its test failed.
- Forgetting that a channel that worked at a hundred customers may not work at ten thousand.
