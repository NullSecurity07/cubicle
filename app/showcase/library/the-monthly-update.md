# The monthly update

*How to write the monthly note to investors and advisers. Written for this app in our own words from YC's Aaron
Harris, as reported by Visible
(https://visible.vc/blog/tips-from-yc-using-asks-metrics-and-a-recap-to-power-your-investor-updates/), and Tom
Blomfield's YC talk on metrics (https://www.ycombinator.com/library/KR-key-startup-metrics).*

## Why send it

An update keeps people who can help you informed without a meeting. It also makes you look at your numbers every
month in front of someone. Founders who send them steadily are easier to back again and easier to introduce.

## Same numbers, same order

Numbers go first. Harris: lead with the metrics that show growth and financial health (revenue, cash in the bank,
burn, growth rate), define them once, and repeat them every month so readers can compare. Blomfield adds that revenue,
net burn and runway belong at the top of every update.

## Honest numbers

A number that fell stays where it was, with one line of why. Blomfield tells of a founder who sent ten updates in a
row with zero revenue at the top: it kept her honest and showed her investors what needed fixing. Leaving a bad
number out tells readers you are hiding something.

## Asks that can be answered

Asks go near the top, so readers who stop early still see them. An answerable ask names what you need, who could
help, and what a yes looks like: an introduction to a specific kind of buyer, a candidate for a named role, advice
on one decision. Three at most. "Let me know if you can help" gets nothing.

## Short recap

The qualitative part is last and short: the few things that mattered this month, wins and misses both, and what next
month is for.

## Shape of the email

One-line summary, numbers, asks, recap, thanks. Under a page. Same day each month.

## What to leave out

Long product news, press mentions that did not bring customers, and anything a reader cannot act on or learn from.
If a paragraph would not change what an investor thinks or does, cut it. (This section is this app's advice, drawn
from Harris's point that the recap holds only what is vital.)

## A worked shape

An example of the shape, with made-up figures: "October: revenue up 12% to $18.4k MRR, runway 14 months." Then the
table of the same five numbers as September. Then two asks: an introduction to a head of operations at a regional
logistics company, and a candidate for a first sales hire who has sold to finance teams. Then three lines of wins, two
of misses, and one line on what November is for.
