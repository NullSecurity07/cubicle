# The business on one page

*How to write the whole business on one page and use it to decide what to test next. Written for this app in our own
words from Ash Maurya's "What is Lean Canvas" (https://ashmaurya.com/blog/what-is-lean-canvas, 2024) and LEANSpark's
canvas guide (https://leanspark.ai/leancanvas).*

## Why one page

A business plan takes weeks and is out of date the day it is done. One page takes minutes and can be redone every time
you learn something. The small boxes force you to say what matters.

## Nine boxes

Customer segments; problem; unique value proposition; solution; channels; revenue streams; cost structure; key
metrics; unfair advantage. Compared with the Business Model Canvas it came from, Lean Canvas swaps in problem,
solution, key metrics and unfair advantage, because a new business must understand the problem before it worries
about partners.

## The sub-boxes

- Early adopters, inside customer segments: the specific people who will try it first.
- Existing alternatives, inside problem: how people cope today.
- High-level concept, inside the value proposition: an "X for Y" a stranger gets at once.

## Twenty minutes

Maurya's advice: do the first one in under twenty minutes. Start with the customer and the problem, then the value
proposition and solution, then channels, metrics, revenue and costs, and leave unfair advantage for last. It is fine
to leave a box blank. A snapshot now beats a perfect plan later.

## Unfair advantage

Something that cannot easily be copied or bought: insider knowledge, a community, existing customers, a team nobody
else has. Most new companies do not have one yet, and "none yet" is an honest answer.

## Riskiest first

Every box you have no evidence for is a guess. Rank the guesses by how badly the business breaks if each is wrong.
Customer and problem usually come first: if nobody has the problem, nothing else on the page matters.

## A test a week

Take the riskiest guess and design one test you can finish this week, with a pass line written before you start.
Update the canvas with what you learn and keep the old version, so you can see how the business changed.

## Key metrics on the page

Three to five numbers that show whether the business is working, not everything you can count. Maurya treats them as
leading indicators. If the Weekly metrics framework is in use, its confirmed primary KPI goes here, word for word.

## When to redo it

Redo the page when a test changes a box, when you talk to a new kind of customer, or when you consider a pivot. Keep
each version; putting two canvases side by side shows what you really learned. Maurya describes the canvas as
something you keep revising as customers teach you, not a document you finish.
